## Fact sheet (every fact with source number[s])

### The Diamond Centre and the vault
- The target was the Antwerp Diamond Centre at Schupstraat 9/11, in the diamond district [14][15][18]. Guinness calls it the "Antwerp World Diamond Centre" [17]. IDEX calls it the "Grunberger building" [8].
- Building size, DISPUTED: 13 floors and 225 dealer offices [15]; "14-story building with a private security force" [16].
- The district had a special police station, steel pillars (vehicle barriers) at both ends and multi-angle cameras [5][9]. Annual turnover was $23bn [5].
- The vault is two floors underground [1][5][18].
- Door: a 3-ton steel door [1][16], about a foot or 12 inches thick [5][9]. The door alone had six layers of security [1][16].
- Combination wheel numbered 0-99, giving "100 million possible combinations" [1].
- "10 layers of security" [1][17]. Wired's diagram lists door items (combination dial, keyed lock, seismic sensor, magnetic sensor, locked steel grate, external camera) and vault items (keypad, light sensor, internal camera, heat/motion sensor) [1]. The text also names infrared heat detectors and Doppler radar [1][18].
- The key: Wired calls it "the nearly impossible-to-duplicate foot-long vault key" [1]. Others describe a "long-necked key" [9] and "a long two-part, 3D key" [14][18].
- Two magnetic plates alarmed an off-site firm if separated outside business hours [9]. A seismic alarm detected drilling or sawing [9].
- During the day the vault door stood open behind a steel grate, which a guard unlocked remotely [16].
- Boxes, DISPUTED: 189 safe deposit boxes [1][9][13][14][18] vs 160 vaults or safes [3][4][6][8][12]. Each box had a key lock plus a three-letter combination, with 17,576 possible combinations [1][14][18].
- Cameras: 24 in the building; the tapes for 15 and 16 Feb went missing [15]. Cameras were tampered with or taped and the videotapes removed [4][5]. Only one guard was on duty that weekend [5].

### Notarbartolo's cover
- Leonardo Notarbartolo was 51 in 2003 [5] and from the Turin area [10][13].
- He rented an office in November 2000 under a phantom company's name [5]. Wired also says 2000, posing as "a gem importer based in Turin" who bought small stones [1][2]. DISPUTED planning length: "two years" [6][9][14][18], "four years" [12][17], "18 months" of surveillance [14]. (Our brief said ~2001; the sources say 2000.)
- Tenancy came with a safe deposit box in the vault [14][18]. He went into the vault with his tenant ID and opened his own box [16].
- Surveillance: a pen with a hidden camera to scout the district [16]. A fingertip-sized camera hidden by a light above the vault door, recording to a device inside a red fire extinguisher [1][18].
- Replica vault: per Notarbartolo, a dealer built "an exact replica of the Diamond Center's vault level" in an abandoned warehouse from his photos, five months later [1]. This rests only on Notarbartolo's own account (see below).
- Who hired whom, DISPUTED and his own claim only: a diamond dealer offered "an initial payment of 100,000 euros" to assess the vault, financed the job and was to get a third of the loot [1][13]. No third party has ever been found [14]. In the 2025 doc he says the job was coordinated by a man named "Alessandro" and that his own role was minor [15].

### The crew ("The School of Turin" / La Scuola di Torino)
- Nicknames come from Notarbartolo, who did not name them; the identities are police conclusions [1][13][14][18].
- "The Genius", alarms and electronics: police say Elio D'Onorio [13][14][18]. His business card was in the trash [1].
- "The Monster", lock picker, electrician, mechanic and driver of "enormous physical strength" [1]: police say Ferdinando Finotto [1][13][14].
- "The King of Keys", "a quiet older man", "among the best key forgers in the world" [1]. Never identified or charged [13][18].
- "Speedy", a nervous childhood friend prone to "coming apart" [1]: police say Pietro Tavano [13][14][18].
- Crew size, DISPUTED: five men [1][13][14] vs "at least eight conspirators" per Belgian police in *Flawless* [9].
- Italian authorities linked Notarbartolo's cousin Benedetto Capizzi, an alleged Mafia figure; Notarbartolo denies any mob link [13]. Prosecutors alleged Mafia ties [6].

### The night (Sat 15 to Sun 16 Feb 2003)
- Fri 14 Feb: Notarbartolo went into the vault alone and sprayed women's hairspray on the heat/motion sensor. The oily film was meant to insulate it [2][14][18].
- Entry was a little past midnight on Saturday night, through an unwatched private garden, a 2nd-floor terrace and a window [1].
- Infrared detector on the terrace: beaten with a homemade polyester shield [1]. Wikipedia says polystyrene [18].
- Magnetic sensor: the Genius moved the plates with "a custom-made slab of rigid aluminum" [1]. Screws had been loosened beforehand and held with tape [14][18].
- Key, DISPUTED: Wired says the original key was "hanging" in a utility/storage room and they used it [1]. AP says the bags held "copied pass keys" and that the thieves "obtained copies of the master keys" [5].
- Light sensor: taped over by the Monster, with the lights switched off before the door opened [1][18]. Heat sensor inside: blinded with a Styrofoam box [1].
- Officials said there was no forced entry [3] and that the thieves knew the entry code [4].
- Boxes opened, DISPUTED: 109 boxes by 5:30 am [1][13][14][18] vs 123 [3][4][6][8][12]. They used a hand-cranked custom box opener [18]. Stones were dropped on the vault floor [3][6][12].
- Discovery: Monday 17 Feb, by a caretaker [4] or by staff [15].

### The loot
- Police and insurers: about $100m [1][2][5][6][17]. De Bruycker counted at least $75m on top of about $25m in claims backed by invoices (adjuster Denice Oliver) [2]. Other estimates: "100 to 500 million dollars" [15]; "nearly half a billion" (Selby site) [20].
- DISPUTED: Notarbartolo says the satchels held only about $20m (EUR 18m), and "We've been set up". He suspects an insurance scam [1][18]. *Flawless* "persuasively debunk[s]" his account [9]. Wikipedia says the vault had been refused insurance, which would make fraud impractical [18].
- Much of the trade runs off the books: "three-quarters" according to De Bruycker [2], while magistrate Leen Nuyts said "The biggest part of the diamond trade is done without a single piece of paper" [5].
- The diamonds were never recovered [6][8][12][13].

### The unravel
- Sunday evening, around 6 pm on the E19 between Antwerp and Brussels, Speedy panicked and dumped a bag in a strip of forest with Notarbartolo present [1][14][18]. AP says bags were thrown "into a ditch beside the highway" [5].
- Landowner August Van Camp, a 59-year-old retired grocer who hated littering, found it on Mon 17 Feb and called the police [1][14][18].
- Contents: videotape, a wine bottle, "a half-eaten salami sandwich", Diamond Center envelopes [1]; break-in tools and copied keys [5]; some emeralds and a supermarket receipt [14]; a torn invoice for a surveillance system bought by Notarbartolo; D'Onorio's business card [1].
- DISPUTED forensics: a receipt for Antipasto Italiano salami led to store CCTV of Finotto [1]. Others say there was DNA on the sandwich [17][18], "bearing Notarbartolo's DNA" [12].
- Lead detectives were Agim De Bruycker and Patrick Peys of Antwerp's Diamond Squad [1][14][15]. De Bruycker to the alarm firm: "Then how is it that the door is wide open and I'm standing inside the vault?" [1]
- Arrest: Notarbartolo came back to the Centre and a guard alerted police [5]. He came to collect mail [1] and was spotted on CCTV by the manager [14]. The date is 21 Feb 2003 per [18] only; others say "about a week" after the theft [6][14].
- His friend Antonio Faletti fled and was caught at Notarbartolo's flat. Notarbartolo's wife Adriana Crudo was also arrested, and both denied involvement [5]. Italian police found 17 polished diamonds in his Turin safe [1].
- Sentence: the Antwerp appeal court ruled on 20 May 2005 [15]. Notarbartolo got 10 years and a EUR 1m fine. Finotto, D'Onorio and Tavano got 5 years and EUR 5,000 each, plus EUR 4.5m in joint damages [7][8][15]. Per People, Finotto was arrested in Italy and D'Onorio extradited in Nov 2007 [13]. *Flawless* says three "temporarily evaded" capture via Italian bureaucracy [9].
- Parole: he left Hasselt prison in March 2009 [11]. DISPUTED time served: "six years" [9][10][11] vs "four years" [8][19]. (He was in custody from Feb 2003, so six years is more likely.)
- 14 Jul 2009: Milan police stopped his BMW and found about 1 kg of stones (1,015 g) in 21 packets [8][10]. His lawyer called them legally bought scrap worth about EUR 10,000-18,000 [8][10]. Business AM says victims recognised some stones [11]. UNRESOLVED.
- 2011: European Arrest Warrant for breaking parole (no compensation to victims, no Italian address given) [11][18][19].
- 2013: arrested at Paris CDG flying back from the US via Washington after a film-rights trip; held in Paris pending extradition as of 12 Feb 2013 [11]. "January 2013" per [19] only. Released 2017 [14][18][19].
- No 2019 airport arrest found: Wikipedia lists no 2019 event [18]. DO NOT USE.
- Today: lives near Turin, reportedly making wood pellets [13][14]. In the Netflix doc (8 Aug 2025) he said he had "always wanted to be part of something like this" [14].
- Later, De Bruycker was prosecuted (2015-16) after rough diamonds were found at his home, and was dismissed from the police [15]. Sensitive and single-sourced; avoid in the script.
