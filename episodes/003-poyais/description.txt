In 1822 and 1823, hundreds of settlers, many of them Scottish, sailed to a capital city with a cathedral, a bank and an opera house. When they landed, there was only jungle.

This is the true story of Gregor MacGregor and Poyais, the con man who invented a country. A Scottish soldier who had fought beside Simón Bolívar came home with a real land grant on the Mosquito Coast of Central America, and turned it into an imaginary nation: a flag, a coat of arms, an army, a guidebook full of gold-filled rivers, and a capital called St Joseph that did not exist.

He sold Poyais land by the acre in London, Edinburgh and Glasgow. A respectable City bank floated a £200,000 loan for the "government of Poyais", and its bonds traded on the London Stock Exchange. Settlers swapped real money for Poyais banknotes. Then two ships sailed, and the settlers went looking for their new home.

What they found, who was blamed, and what happened to the man who sold it, is one of the strangest stories in the history of fraud.
