# Sources: 005 How One Man Stole the Mona Lisa With a Plumber's Help (Vincenzo Peruggia, 1911)

## Sources read
1. "Stolen: How the Mona Lisa Became the World's Most Famous Painting", James Zug, Smithsonian Magazine, https://www.smithsonianmag.com/arts-culture/stolen-how-the-mona-lisa-became-the-worlds-most-famous-painting-16406234/. Monday 21 Aug 1911, Louvre closed on Mondays; Peruggia plus the Lancelotti brothers hid overnight Sunday in a storeroom near the Salon Carré in white smocks; glass shadow box and frame removed; back stairwell; 26 hours before anyone noticed. Louvre: 1,000+ rooms, 45 acres, fewer than 150 guards, about a quarter-million objects. Cobier glaziers put about a dozen paintings under glass after a 1910 threat; Peruggia was one of them. Born in Dumenza, 5 ft 3. Questioned Nov 1911, said he overslept after drinking. Picasso and Apollinaire held and released. Trunk with a false bottom in his boardinghouse room; 28 months; Florence, Dec 1913; dealer called police; eight months served. Empty wall with "four lonely iron hooks"; Kafka and Max Brod visited. Mobbed shows in Florence, Milan and Rome; 100,000+ visitors in two days after return; 8 million a year today. Baedeker 1878: one paragraph; 1907: two sentences. Valfierno/Decker forgery story: "no supporting historical evidence". High reliability.
2. "The Heist That Made the Mona Lisa Famous", Evan Andrews, HISTORY.com (2016, updated 2026), https://www.history.com/news/the-heist-that-made-the-mona-lisa-famous. Hid in a storage closet Sunday evening 20 Aug; came out about 7:15 am in a white smock/apron; locked stairwell door, tried to take the doorknob apart, a Louvre plumber took him for a co-worker and opened it; noticed around noon Tuesday by a visiting artist; "The Mona Lisa is gone"; reopened a week later, thousands came to see the empty wall; rumours (J.P. Morgan, the Germans); Apollinaire arrested Sept 1911, Picasso questioned; trunk with a false bottom; quote "I fell a victim to her smile and feasted my eyes on my treasure every evening"; letter to Alfredo Geri signed "Leonard"; Geri and Uffizi director Giovanni Poggi; painting wrapped in red silk; 500,000 lire; arrested 11 Dec 1913 at his hotel; returned Jan 1914; patriotism defence vs Francis I's legal purchase; sentence one year and 15 days, served seven months; WWI, died 1925; at least 120,000 visitors in two days; about 8 million a year, bulletproof glass. High reliability.

Three men in smocks, about 7 am Monday, holiday closure, slept in the storeroom; ditched the case and frame; "incredibly famous painting literally overnight" (James Zug); trunk in a boarding-house room; Poggi checked a stamp on the back; pleaded guilty, eight months. High reliability.

5. "Vincenzo Peruggia", Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincenzo_Peruggia. Pointer-level reliability; used where it agrees with 1-4 or hedged.
6. "The Mona Lisa is stolen from the Louvre", History Today (Months Past), https://www.historytoday.com/archive/months-past/mona-lisa-stolen-louvre. Petty criminal who worked at the Louvre for a time; letter signed "Leonardo Vincenzo" offering it for 500,000 lire; trunk with a false bottom; the hotel later renamed itself the Hotel La Gioconda; Apollinaire held a week, Picasso suspected; crowds at the Uffizi weeping with joy; patriotism "what he said, at least". High reliability.

## How the script handles disputes
- One thief or three: Smithsonian and NPR say Peruggia had two helpers (the Lancelotti brothers); HISTORY.com and Wikipedia tell it as one man. The script says "by some accounts, two brothers helped him".
- Overnight hide vs walking in at 7 am: Smithsonian/HISTORY/NPR say he hid in a storeroom overnight; his own Florence account (Wikipedia) has him walk in at 7 am. The script gives both.
- The plumber appears in HISTORY.com and Wikipedia (not in the Smithsonian, NPR or History Today pieces). Both agree on it, so the script tells it plainly.
- Painting "under the smock": Wikipedia doubts it; the script says he wrapped it in a white cloth.
- Sentence: one year and fifteen days, about seven months served (HISTORY x2, Wikipedia); NPR and Smithsonian say eight months. The script says "seven months, by most accounts".
- Picasso: arrested (Wikipedia) vs questioned (HISTORY.com). The script says "brought in for questioning".
- Signature on the letter: "Leonard", "Leonardo", "Leonardo V.", "Leonardo Vincenzo". The script says "signs it, Leonardo".
- "26 hours" (Smithsonian) appears only on a thumbnail variant; the narration says "more than twenty-four hours" / "more than a day".
