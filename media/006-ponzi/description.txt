On a hot Monday in July 1920, a line of frightened investors stretched down School Street in Boston, and a smiling man in a straw hat handed out coffee while his clerks paid them back in cash.

This is the true story of Charles Ponzi and the original Ponzi scheme. He arrived in Boston in 1903 with two dollars and fifty cents, worked his way through dishwashing jobs, a forged cheque and two prison terms, and then, in 1919, found a small slip of paper in a letter from Spain: an international reply coupon worth five cents in America.

His promise was simple: fifty per cent profit in forty-five days. His Securities Exchange Company took in an estimated fifteen million dollars in about eight months. He bought a mansion, a limousine and even the bank that had once refused him a loan. But the coupons were only the story. Step by step, this video shows how the trick really worked, why the line had to grow faster and faster, and how a newspaper, a publicity man, a financial journalist and a tip from Montreal brought it all down.
